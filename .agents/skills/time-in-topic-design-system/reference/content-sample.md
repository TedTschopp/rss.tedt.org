# Yesterday in AI — Reference Content Sample

Source URLs:

## Observed structure

Each daily edition has these sections (in order):
1. **TL;DR** — 3–5 bullet summary of the day
2. **Big Moves** — major announcements, deals, releases
3. **Enterprise Ops** — operational/customer stories from real companies
4. **Models & Tooling (Vendors)** — model releases, SDK changes, APIs
5. **Security & Trust** — incidents, safety research, mitigations
6. **Policy & Geopolitics** — regulation, sovereign AI, government partnerships
7. **Quick Hits** — short bullet briefs
8. **Looking Ahead** — what to watch tomorrow / this week
9. **Sign-off** — short editorial closer

## Story shape (the most important pattern)

Every story below the TL;DR follows this structure:

```
### {Headline — sentence case, declarative}
{1–2 sentence "Why it matters" / context paragraph in plain prose.}

**As a leader:** {What an exec / decision-maker should do or watch.}
**As an individual:** {What an IC / practitioner should do.}

Source: {Publisher} — {short link title}
```

## Voice / tone characteristics (drawn from feed sample)

- Authoritative but not breathless. No "BREAKING" or hype.
- Sentence-case headlines, not Title Case.
- Direct, factual ledes: "OpenAI launches the EU Economic Blueprint 2.0…"
- Mild wryness allowed in transitions ("clarifying about a week of confusion")
- Specific numbers used liberally: "$40.5M", "750MW", "1 million business customers"
- Mixes business outcomes ("cut HR analysis time by 90%") with technical specifics
- Avoids first-person; "we" only in editorial sign-off
- Names companies and people directly; no euphemisms
