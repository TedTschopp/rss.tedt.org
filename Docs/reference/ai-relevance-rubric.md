# AI Relevance Rubric

Use this rubric as a gate before enterprise importance grading.

## Proceed

Classify the story as AI-related when the title, summary, or article content is substantively about one or more of the following:

- Artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, neural networks, foundation models, or large language models.
- AI products, assistants, agents, copilots, inference platforms, model serving, retrieval, embeddings, fine-tuning, evaluation, safety, or governance.
- Enterprise adoption, regulation, security, operations, labor impact, economics, infrastructure, or vendor strategy where AI capability is a material part of the development.
- AI research, benchmarks, model releases, datasets, chips, accelerators, data centers, power demand, or platform dependencies when tied to AI workloads.

## Skip

Classify the story as not AI-related when AI is only incidental, decorative, ambiguous, or absent.

Skip examples:

- A product name, marketing label, stock ticker, or acronym contains "AI" but the article is not about artificial intelligence.
- The article discusses general software, cloud, security, hardware, gaming, finance, media, or workplace news without a material AI component.
- The source page, sidebar, advertisements, or unrelated links mention AI, but the story itself does not.
- The story is purely about a person, company, or market unless the development materially concerns AI capability, governance, deployment, or impact.

## Decision Rules

- Evaluate the title, summary, and article content together.
- Prefer the article content when it conflicts with the title or summary.
- If evidence is weak, ambiguous, or only keyword-based, choose `skip`.
- Choose `proceed` only when a reasonable enterprise technology reader would say the development is actually about AI.
